We're bringing **Projects** and **Client Onboarding** into one workspace. Your team will manage onboarding alongside project delivery, with shared templates and settings under **Settings > Projects**.

Your existing onboardings will carry forward. You won't need to rebuild your templates, restart active portals, or send replacement client invitations because of this migration.

This notice is for **MSP administrators and staff**. If you have clients working through an onboarding portal, the [client impact](#what-your-clients-will-experience) section explains what to tell them.

## Why we're making this change

Onboarding often involves the same people and work as project delivery: phases, tasks, due dates, and client input. Bringing them together gives your team one place to find that work while keeping the client-facing onboarding experience available.

You'll be able to find onboarding work from **Projects > Onboarding**, open its linked project, and manage the portal from that workspace. Project types will identify which workflows use onboarding features.

## What will move

| What you use today | Where you'll find it after the update |
| --- | --- |
| A company's onboarding overview | The linked project's **Onboarding** tab, accessible from **Projects > Onboarding** |
| Onboarding tasks, client document requests, pages, activity, dates, and portal settings | The project's **Client Portal** tab |
| Onboarding templates | **Settings > Projects > Templates** |
| Reusable onboarding checklists | **Settings > Projects > Templates > Checklists** |
| Onboarding form definitions | **Settings > Projects > Forms** |
| Portal directory, client roles, reusable pages, and supported PSA project defaults | **Settings > Projects > Client Portal** |
| The choice of whether a project type uses onboarding features | **Settings > Projects > Project Types**, using the **Onboarding** switch |

The tabs you see depend on your role and the project. If a project tab isn't in the main row, check **More**. **More > Customize view** lets you choose and order your own project tabs without changing anyone else's access.

Existing staff bookmarks to company onboarding pages will open the corresponding project section once that portal has a linked project and the update is enabled. Onboarding settings bookmarks will redirect to their new location under **Settings > Projects**.

## What happens to your existing work

### Onboardings already linked to a project

An existing link to a PSA or MSPortal project is retained. The migration uses that project rather than creating a replacement. Your onboarding portal remains attached to it.

### Onboardings without a linked project

MSPortal automatically creates and links an **MSPortal project** using the portal's name and company. It brings across the portal's phases and creates project tasks for MSP-owned onboarding tasks that don't already have a ticket link. Client-owned tasks stay in the onboarding portal.

This can make additional projects appear in your portfolio. These are the project counterparts of existing onboardings. The automatic migration does **not** create projects or tickets in your external PSA.

For these newly created projects, completed, converted, or archived portals produce closed projects. If you're looking for historical onboarding work, include **Closed** or **All** in your Projects view.

### Portals, templates, and progress

The migration retains the existing portal records, contacts, task progress, submissions, and portal configuration. Your reusable templates remain available through the shared template library.

You do not need to export and reimport your onboarding data or recreate active portals. Normal portal lifecycle rules still apply: a completed or archived portal does not become active again because it appears in Projects.

## What your clients will experience

The main navigation change is for your team. Clients can continue using their existing onboarding links and completing work in the onboarding portal. This migration does not require them to create a new account or receive a replacement invitation.

Existing link expiry, revoked access, and portal status restrictions still apply. If a client already cannot access a portal, the migration does not restore that access.

Moving onboarding into Projects does not give clients access to every project or staff workspace. Company access, role permissions, and portal invitations continue to control what they can see.

<Info>
**Project Documents and client document requests are separate.** The project's **Documents** tab is for MSP staff. Client uploads and document requests remain under **Client Portal > Documents**. Uploading a staff document does not share it with clients.
</Info>

Forms can also be used on regular projects without an onboarding portal. Staff must explicitly share a project form before clients can access it, and the client role needs the relevant form permissions. Existing onboarding forms attached to portal tasks remain part of that workflow.

## How to prepare now

There is no manual data migration for your team to perform. We recommend these checks before the navigation changes:

1. **Brief the people who manage onboarding.** Share this page with project managers, onboarding coordinators, and administrators. Highlight the new locations for day-to-day work and reusable settings.
2. **Identify the active onboardings you will check afterward.** Note the company, portal name, and any linked project so your team knows what to look for in Projects.
3. **Review your staff roles.** Confirm who needs to view projects, run a client portal, and edit reusable onboarding settings. These are separate capabilities; project access alone does not grant every onboarding administration action. Keep existing onboarding permissions in place.
4. **Identify internal instructions that need updating.** Check your runbooks and saved staff bookmarks for references to **Settings > Onboarding** or the company onboarding workspace. Use the table above when updating them after rollout.
5. **Keep active client onboarding running.** Continue using current portals and invitations. There is no need to pause an onboarding or ask clients to start again for this change.

## What to check after the update

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find an existing onboarding">
    Open **Projects > Onboarding**, select the relevant company scope, and find one of your active onboardings. Open its project and check the **Onboarding** and **Client Portal** tabs. Use **More** if a tab isn't in the main row.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the work in progress">
    Confirm the portal name, contacts, phases, task progress, and client submissions. For a portal that previously had no project, expect an MSPortal project with the same portal name. Use **Closed** or **All** when checking historical work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check templates and settings">
    Open **Settings > Projects** and locate your templates, forms, and client portal settings. Review **Project Types** to confirm which types should use onboarding features.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify access with your usual staff role">
    Check that the people responsible for onboarding can find and manage the portal. If a tab is missing or an action is unavailable, have an administrator review the relevant project, portal, and onboarding-settings permissions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update your team's instructions">
    Update staff bookmarks and runbooks to use the new locations. Existing client onboarding links can stay in place.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do we need to migrate anything ourselves?">
    No. MSPortal handles linking existing portals and creating MSPortal projects where a portal has no linked project. Your team's preparation is to review navigation and access, then check active work after the update.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do we need to rebuild our templates?">
    No. Existing templates remain available in **Settings > Projects > Templates**. Review their project type when you next edit them so they use the intended onboarding or regular-project workflow.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will this create duplicate projects in our PSA?">
    The automatic migration creates no external PSA projects or tickets. Already-linked portals keep their project link. Unlinked portals receive an MSPortal project. Any later PSA linking or creation you choose is a separate action.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will every project become an onboarding?">
    No. Regular projects remain regular projects. Onboarding features are associated with linked onboarding portals and project types configured for onboarding.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why can't I see an onboarding after the update?">
    Check the selected company, search and filters, and whether the project is closed. Also check **More** for hidden project tabs. If the work is still missing or access is denied, ask your administrator to review your permissions or contact MSPortal support with the company and portal name.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="We only use Projects. Does this affect us?">
    You'll see the consolidated settings available to your role. You don't need to create onboarding portals to keep using regular projects. The onboarding-specific migration applies to existing onboarding portals.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can we keep using the current workflow until rollout?">
    Yes. Continue using the navigation currently available in your organization. This page describes the upcoming experience, so your menus may not match it yet.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
