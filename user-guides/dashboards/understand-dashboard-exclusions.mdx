Your MSP can hide selected submitters, ticket types and recurring tickets from supported dashboard widgets. The settings apply across the tenant, including Company views. They do not delete tickets or change their status in your support system. This guide is for Company users and MSP staff who read dashboards.

## What can be filtered

| Setting | Where it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Excluded Submitters** | Submitter charts such as Top Ticket Submitters. It can hide generic labels or specific names. |
| **Excluded Ticket Types** | Ticket type breakdown charts. Other dashboard totals still count those tickets. |
| **Exclude Recurring Tickets** | Supported ticket widgets and their drilldowns omit tickets created from PSA recurring ticket templates. Detection covers the Autotask recurring ticket format only. |

A chart can differ from the main Tickets list because it uses its own dates, company, metric definition and exclusions. A top-submitters chart also shows only a limited number of names. A missing label does not mean tickets were lost.

## Investigate an unexpected count

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the dashboard context">
    Confirm the dashboard, the company and the dates. Read the widget label. Different charts can use different periods.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect the tickets">
    Click a supported chart segment or summary card to open its ticket list. Compare the same company and dates. See [Inspect dashboard tickets](/user-guides/dashboards/inspect-dashboard-tickets).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask your MSP about exclusions">
    Give the MSP the dashboard name, the widget, the dates and the label or ticket you expected. Ask if a submitter, ticket type, recurring ticket rule or user access setting explains the difference.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Company users cannot change exclusions or permissions. MSP staff with Dashboards **Write** or **Manage** access under Settings can review **Settings > Dashboards > Exclusions**.

## What exclusions do not do

Exclusions are display filters for the widgets that use them. They are not access controls, they do not stop recurring tickets in your PSA, and they do not change external dashboards. Other pages and reports use their own rules. If your concern is access to sensitive records, ask your MSP to review user permissions.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| A submitter is missing from a chart | Ask your MSP if that name is excluded. Also check the company, dates and the top-results limit. |
| A widget shows fewer tickets than the Tickets list | Compare dates, company, role visibility and exclusions. |
| Recurring tickets are missing from a widget | Ask your MSP if **Exclude Recurring Tickets** is on, or if your role hides recurring tickets in its own ticket view. |

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do dashboard exclusions delete or close tickets?">
    No. They filter widget data only.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I change exclusions just for my company?">
    No. They are tenant-wide MSP settings. Ask your MSP.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does excluding a ticket type remove it from every dashboard total?">
    No. It applies to type breakdown charts.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Exclude Recurring Tickets stop new recurring tickets?">
    No. It filters supported dashboard data. Your PSA's schedule is unchanged.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Are recurring tickets from every PSA detected?">
    No. Detection covers the Autotask recurring ticket format.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can exclusions protect confidential ticket data?">
    No. They are display filters, not permissions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What should I send my MSP about a missing value?">
    The dashboard name, widget, company, dates and the ticket or label you expected.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
