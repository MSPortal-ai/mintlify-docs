This page is for MSP staff who manage the Standards catalog. It shows how to set conditions on a standard so it applies only to the companies it fits. Applicability answers "should this client have this standard?" It does not change whether a tool is installed or whether a deployment is done.

## Before you start

- You need the permission to manage Standards settings. Your billing must be active to save.
- To use company fields in a condition, you also need read access to custom fields.
- To change a company's industry from the company table, you need write access to company settings. To change a company field there, you also need write access to custom fields.
- Company computer platforms come from active device inventory. There is no manual PC or Mac setting for a company. Set the conditions on each standard instead.

## Add conditions to a standard

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the standard">
    Go to **Settings > MSP Tools > Standards**, expand the category, and select the standard's name to open its editor. Find the **Standards applicability** row.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a condition">
    Select **Add condition**. Choose the **Condition** to test, a **Comparison** such as **is any of**, and the **Value**. A new standard applies to every company until you add a condition. You can add up to 20 conditions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Combine conditions">
    With two or more conditions, choose **all conditions match** or **any condition matches**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let it save">
    There is no Save button. A complete rule saves a moment after your last change and shows **Conditions saved**. If a condition is incomplete, such as **is any of** with no values, finish it or remove it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the matrix">
    Open the [Standards matrix](/user-guides/msp-tools/read-standards-matrix). Companies that do not match show the standard as a locked N/A.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Condition | What it tests |
| --- | --- |
| **Company computer platforms** | Active PCs and Macs that belong to the company. Use **has any of** or **has none of**. |
| **Company device types** | Active devices of the selected types that belong to the company. Use **has any of** or **has none of**. |
| **Industry** | The company's industry. |
| **Company type** | The company's PSA company type. |
| **Standards tier** | The tier mapped to the company's type. |
| **Company fields** | Any company custom field. Option fields use **is any of** or **is none of**. Text fields support **contains**. Number fields support **is greater than** and **is less than**. Date fields support **is on**, **is after** and **is before**. |

## Target companies by their computers

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a platform condition">
    Add a condition and choose **Company computer platforms**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the platforms">
    Choose **has any of** and select **PCs**, **Macs**, or both.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Require both, if needed">
    To require both kinds of computer, add one PC condition and one Mac condition, and choose **all conditions match**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="An unsaved computer-platform condition and its available values.">
  <img src="/images/product/standards-inventory-platform-preview.png" alt="Authenticated editor showing Company computer platforms and the PC and Mac value choices." width="2880" height="1920" />
</Frame>

- **has any of PCs** includes PC-only and mixed companies.
- **has any of Macs** includes Mac-only and mixed companies.
- Windows servers and inactive devices are ignored. A company with no active computers does not match **has any of PCs** or **has any of Macs**.
- **has none of** matches when none of the selected platforms are present, including when the company has no active inventory.

Rules saved before this change still work. Platform results now follow active inventory, so a company with no active computers can become N/A where the old automatic fallback treated it as having PCs and Macs.

## Target companies by device types

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a device type condition">
    Add a condition and choose **Company device types**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the types">
    Choose **has any of** or **has none of**, then select types such as workstations, servers or network devices from the inventory catalog.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## See company facts in one table

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the table">
    Go to **Settings > MSP Tools > Standards** and select the **Company Standards applicability** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Narrow the list">
    Use the company selector and the table filters to show the companies you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the columns">
    Check each company's computer platforms, device types, industry, company type, tier, and company fields.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Platform and device-type columns are read-only. You can edit **Industry** and company fields in the table when you have the permissions listed above.

## Handle missing values

For metadata conditions such as Industry or company fields, a company with no value keeps the standard. This stops incomplete records from quietly raising a compliance percentage.

To target companies with no value, use **is empty** or **is not empty**. For example, **Industry is not empty** removes companies whose industry was never set.

Inventory conditions work on presence instead. An empty inventory fails **has any of** and satisfies **has none of**.

## Know what N/A and scoring do

- A rule-driven N/A cell is locked. Changing a deployment status cannot override it.
- N/A cells do not count as compliance gaps. They leave the Standards compliance calculation and the matching Client Health scoring.
- The deployment answer saved earlier is kept. If a later rule change makes the standard apply again, the saved answer shows again.
- Client Health can show an earlier scoring run. Compare the scope and the refresh time.

Service plans are a separate setting. Each standard's editor has a **Service plans** field. An empty selection means **All plans**. In the matrix, a standard outside a company's plan is dimmed with a tooltip, but plan membership does not reduce the compliance denominator.

Compliance checks use the same conditions, plus device conditions. See [Compliance Settings](/user-guides/settings/compliance#choosing-which-companies-and-devices-get-a-check).

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The **Standards applicability** row is missing | Check that you have the permission to manage Standards settings. |
| A company is missing from the table | Check the company selector and your permitted company scope. |
| A company field is missing from the condition list | Check that you have read access to custom fields. User fields cannot be used in conditions. |
| A company with no value still has the standard | This is intended. Fill in the value, or use **is empty** or **is not empty**. |
| A cell is locked N/A | Review the standard's **Standards applicability** conditions. A deployment status cannot override them. |
| The rule does not save | Finish or remove incomplete conditions, such as **is any of** with no values. Your billing must also be active. |
| A score went up without new deployments | Excluding a required check changes the number of applicable checks. Review the exclusions next to the percentage. |

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I set a company's platform to PC or Mac by hand?">
    No. Platforms come from active device inventory. Add **Company computer platforms** conditions to each standard.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why is a company with no computers marked N/A?">
    A company with no active computers does not match **has any of PCs** or **has any of Macs**, so those standards become N/A.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I need to press Save?">
    No. A complete rule saves on its own a moment after your last change.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does N/A delete the deployment answer?">
    No. The earlier answer is kept and shows again if the standard applies again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How many conditions can one standard have?">
    Up to 20.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does a service plan make a standard N/A?">
    No. A standard outside a company's plan is dimmed in the matrix but still counts in the compliance percentage.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
